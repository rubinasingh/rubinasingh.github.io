I'm a fourth-year PhD student in Transportation Engineering at the University of Washington, advised by Don MacKenzie. My work sits at the intersection of infrastructure, behavior, and policy — using discrete choice models and program evaluation to ask what actually moves the needle on cleaner, fairer transportation.

Most of my projects start from a practical question: does this charger network reliably charge cars? Does a rebate actually get people out of their car? What do delivery drivers really want from curb pricing? I work with state agencies, randomized trials, and big behavioral datasets to answer them.

Before the PhD I earned my BS in Civil Engineering at UT Austin and worked in preconstruction at DPR Construction. I'm currently chair of UW CEE's Graduate Student Advisory Board and a 2026 CUTC Outstanding Student of the Year for PacTrans.

02   Research

Five threads that keep showing up in my work.

// 01

EV charging infrastructure

How public chargers actually perform — uptime, reliability, station-level user experience — and the way that performance feeds back into who is willing to buy an EV.

// 02

Micromobility use

When and why people pick e-scooters, e-bikes, or shared bikes over driving — and what trip-level and attitudinal factors really drive those choices.

mode choicee-bikesattitudes
// 03

Incentive design

Designing and evaluating rebates and pricing programs — from the WSDOT e-bike rebate lottery to curb pricing for urban freight carriers.

rebatesRCTspricing
// 04

Program evaluation

Causal inference for transportation policy: using randomized rollouts, natural experiments, and longitudinal survey panels to measure what programs do versus what they promise.

causalpanel datapolicy
// 05

Choice modeling

Discrete choice models — multinomial logit, mixed logit, conditional logit — as a lens for understanding decisions across mode, vehicle, charger, and parking choices. I also teach this material to graduate students at UW.
